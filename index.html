Built for shutoff country

Nevada County sits in PG&E public safety power shutoff territory, and a lot of the housing here runs on well pumps, propane and long driveways at the end of the line. That combination is exactly what a standby generator is for.

The shutoff problem

Outages here are planned, and long

In the October 2026 shutoff scope, about 2,137 PG&E customers in Nevada County were in line to lose power, and on one storm day, November 18, outages here peaked at 5,019 customers out. A portable unit and an extension cord do not cover a multi-day shutoff well.

Who it hits hardest

30.4% of the county is 65 or older

That is the county’s age profile, and it is why outages here are more than an inconvenience. Refrigerated medication, medical devices, heat and a working phone line all ride on the same power feed.

Well country

No power often means no water

Outside the town water systems, homes here run on wells. When the power goes, the pump stops, and the taps, toilets and pressure tank go with it. A properly sized standby unit keeps the pump on the list of things that stay live.

Getting ready for the next PG&E shutoff

A PG&E Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) in Nevada County can last days, and fire-weather shutoffs tend to land with little notice. A standby generator covers the power side automatically. These are the things around it that still need a human.

  • Propane topped up before fire season. A standby unit only runs as long as its fuel lasts. A multi-day shutoff at half load can burn through a small tank faster than most people expect.
  • Yearly service done, not overdue. Oil, filters, plugs and battery checked, and a test run under load completed before shutoff season, not during it.
  • Well pump on the covered list. Confirm at sizing time that the pump is on a circuit the generator actually feeds, so water keeps flowing when the grid drops.
  • Medical devices planned for. Refrigerated medication, oxygen and powered medical equipment are the loads to name first at the in-home estimate, they set the sizing floor.
  • Weekly self-test left enabled. Standby units exercise themselves weekly. A failed self-test warning is an early service call, not something to clear and forget.
  • Clearance kept clear. Brush, stored firewood and snow kept away from the unit and its exhaust, especially in wooded areas like Alta Sierra and Penn Valley.
